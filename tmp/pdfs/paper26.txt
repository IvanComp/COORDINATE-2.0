Abstract
Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) consist of autonomous entities that operate individually or collaboratively to complete tasks, relying
on coordination mechanisms to assign responsibilities, manage failures, and regulate agent interactions. Since coordination de-
termines how tasks are allocated, transferred, and recovered during execution, it directly affects the MAS performance objective,
such as reducing task latency or improving task completion under uncertain operating conditions. Yet, coordination mechanisms are
selected at design time and kept fixed during execution. As a result, unexpected changes in workload, agent failures, or communica-
tion losses may degrade MAS performance because its coordination mechanism cannot adapt. This paper addresses this limitation
by framing the selection of coordination mechanisms as a runtime adaptation problem. We propose a dynamic adaptation strategy
that combines stream-based runtime monitoring and online learning. RTLola, a stream-based monitoring language formalism,
computes prespecified MAS performance objectives and tracks evolving operational conditions from the simulation event stream,
while a contextual bandit uses this feedback to select the most efficient among three coordination mechanisms. We implement
the dynamic adaptation strategy in COORDINATE 2.0, a discrete-event simulation tool for configuring, executing, and analyzing
fault-tolerant MAS scenarios. The evaluation, conducted in a smart-hospital scenario, shows that monitoring-guided adaptation can
improve MAS performance under changing workload and fault conditions, while introducing negligible runtime overhead.
Keywords: Multi-Agent Systems, Stream-based Monitoring, Online Learning, Contextual Bandit, Performance Evaluation
