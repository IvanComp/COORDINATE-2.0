Abstract
Multi-Agent Systems (MAS) consist of autonomous entities that operate individually or collaboratively to complete tasks, relying
on coordination mechanisms to assign responsibilities, manage failures, and regulate agent interactions. Since coordination deter-
mines how tasks are allocated, transferred, and recovered during execution, it directly affects the MAS performance objective, such
as reducing task latency or improving task completion under uncertain operating conditions. Yet, coordination mechanisms are
selected at design time and kept fixed during execution. This limits their effectiveness when workload, agent failures, communica-
tion losses, or malicious behaviors change at runtime. This paper addresses this limitation by framing the selection of coordination
mechanisms as a runtime adaptation problem. We propose a dynamic adaptation strategy that combines stream-based runtime mon-
itoring and online learning. RTLola, a stream-based monitoring language formalism, computes prespecified MAS performance
objectives from the simulation event stream, while a contextual bandit uses this monitored feedback together with the current op-
erating context to select among three representative coordination mechanisms. We implement the dynamic adaptation strategy in
COORDINATE 2.0, a discrete-event simulation tool for configuring, executing, and analyzing fault-tolerant MAS scenarios. The
evaluation, conducted in a smart-hospital scenario, shows that monitoring-guided adaptation can improve MAS performance under
changing workload and fault conditions, while introducing negligible runtime overhead.
Keywords: Multi-Agent Systems, Stream-based Monitoring, Online Learning, Contextual Bandit, Performance Evaluation
